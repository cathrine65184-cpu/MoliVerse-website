# Letter World: Love in Letters

An original MoliVerse English course collection by Catherine for ages 6–12.

**Guiding line:** One day, you will understand love a little more.

## What children do

This is a gentle, story-led language journey. Children move through three layers:

1. **Read a story** — enter Letter World with Elia, a young letter keeper who is learning how to name feelings.
2. **Learn expressions** — practise useful English for greetings, memories, feelings, wishes and letter endings.
3. **Write a letter** — create a short, true note for a safe person, a pet, a tree or their future self.

Children never need to share a letter aloud. A drawing, voice note, home language or a few English words all count as expression.

## The original story world

Letter World is an original MoliVerse setting. After a long season of silence, Elia begins work at the Letter House. She discovers that words can carry warmth across distance: a parent’s reminder, a friend’s welcome, a thank-you, an apology, a hopeful wish. Each letter teaches her that care has many shapes.

## Five-part collection

### 01 · A quiet farewell

**Story focus:** Elia hears a caring phrase she does not yet understand.

**Language:** farewell; I feel sad / confused / empty.

**Catherine asks:** “When you hear a word you do not understand, what might you feel inside?”

### 02 · A new beginning

**Story focus:** Elia learns the work of a letter keeper.

**Language:** Dear [Name],; How are you?; a letter; a pen; gentle.

**Catherine asks:** “What words are sometimes easier to write than to say aloud?”

### 03 · Other people’s hearts

**Story focus:** Small acts of care can say a lot.

**Language:** I remember when …; Thank you for …; I feel warm; I miss you.

**Catherine asks:** “What small thing has someone done that made you feel cared for?”

### 04 · A poem for everyone

**Story focus:** Care can be family, friendship, kindness and hope.

**Language:** With love,; You matter.; I hope you …; kind; grateful.

**Catherine asks:** “Love is … ? You can finish the sentence in your own way.”

### 05 · Your letter

**Story focus:** The child becomes a letter keeper.

**Language scaffold:**

Dear [Name],

I remember when [one small thing].

I felt [warm / happy / grateful].

I hope you [wish].

With love,

[Your name]

## Three ways to write

**Starter (ages 6–8): Sandwich letter**

- Top: Dear [Name]
- Filling: I remember when [small moment]
- Bottom: I hope you [wish]. With love, [Name]

**Growing writer (ages 9–12): Five small paragraphs**

1. Greeting
2. One specific moment
3. How it felt inside
4. A thank-you or hope
5. A sign-off

**Bilingual explorer**

Children may draft in their strongest language, then ask Catherine for help with one English sentence. Expression comes before perfection.

## Catherine’s mentoring voice

“I am a gentle letter mentor. I help children read stories slowly, name feelings without judgement, and write one true sentence at a time. I do not grade their feelings or promise a real teacher response. If a child shares a heavy feeling, I pause kindly and invite a trusted grown-up to join the care.”

## Human moments

If a child says “Nobody loves me,” “I do not know love,” “I am scared,” or shares another heavy feeling, Catherine should:

1. Thank the child for telling her.
2. Say clearly that they deserve care and do not have to solve it alone.
3. Invite them to tell a trusted grown-up now (parent, guardian, teacher or another safe adult).
4. Keep the language calm and short; never pressure the child to explain more or promise secret contact.

Suggested language: “I’m really glad you told me. That sounds heavy to carry alone. You deserve care. Can you tell a trusted grown-up near you today? We can pause the letter for now.”

## Educator notes

- Do not require children to share personal letters publicly.
- Correct spelling only with permission; a true message matters more than a perfect sentence.
- Avoid romantic framing. This course explores family care, friendship, empathy, gratitude and hope.
