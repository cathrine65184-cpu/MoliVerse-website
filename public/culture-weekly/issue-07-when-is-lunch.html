When Is Lunch? Different Days in Different Places

Why meal times and rest times change from country to country.

English, with a little Spanish · Read together with a parent or educator

Before we begin

This issue is about daily routines. You can compare it with your own day.

A letter to our readers

Hi, children.

Hi, children. What time do you eat lunch? What time is dinner? In some places, the answers are very different from yours.

Today we look at the day in Spain and at why every place has its own rhythm.

A word we can explain

A routine is the pattern of your day.

A routine is what usually happens, in the same order: waking up, eating, studying, playing, resting and sleeping.

Routines are shaped by weather, work, school and family habits, and they can change from place to place and from year to year.

An idea to wonder about

Dinner at nine?

In Spain, people traditionally eat lunch around 2 p.m. and dinner at 9 p.m. or later. Spain sits much farther west than Germany, but in 1940 it moved its clocks to the same time as Germany and never moved them back. So by the clock, the sun rises and sets about an hour later. Some people think this helps explain the late dinners.

Spain is also known for the siesta, a rest in the middle of the day. Today many people work through the afternoon, and not everyone naps. The siesta is more a tradition than a rule.

Think beyond the clock

Rest is part of a good day.

Whether it is a nap, a quiet hour or a walk, many cultures have a way to rest in the middle of the day. Resting helps us feel calm and ready for more.

No routine is better than another. Each one fits the people who live there.

Talk / Wonder / Make

What do you think?

  1. Talk. What does your day look like from morning to night?
  2. Wonder. Why might meal times change from place to place?
  3. Make. Draw a clock showing your day. Compare it with a friend’s.

Nothing you say or draw is collected here.

It’s okay to take a break.

Rest is always allowed. Stretching or a quiet moment counts.

For families and educators

The siesta is often over-simplified. Habits differ by region, season and job, so please avoid presenting it as how “everyone” lives. Ask children what their own routines are.

Meal times are typical ranges, and the link between the time zone and meal times is a popular theory, not a proven fact.
