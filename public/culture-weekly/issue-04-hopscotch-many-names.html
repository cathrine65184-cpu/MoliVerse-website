Hopscotch Has Many Names

Children everywhere play similar games, with different names.

English, with a little Spanish · Read together with a parent or educator

Before we begin

This issue is about play. Feel free to get up and try the games as you read.

A letter to our readers

Hi, children.

Hi, children. Do you like jumping, racing or rolling marbles? Children in many countries have played games like these for a very long time.

Today we look at a few games and the Spanish words for them.

A word we can explain

Rules are promises that make a game fair.

A game needs rules so everyone knows what to do. When everyone agrees to the same rules, nobody is left out and the game can be fair.

Rules can also change. Children often invent new rules for the same game, and that is part of playing.

An idea to wonder about

One game, many names.

Hopscotch is called rayuela in Spain and Argentina, avioncito (“little airplane”) in Mexico and golosa in Colombia. Jumping rope is saltar la cuerda, and marbles are canicas. Nobody knows exactly where hopscotch began. You may read that it is from ancient Rome, but researchers have not found proof of that.

A game can travel just like a food or a word. A child moves to a new place, teaches the game to new friends, and it grows.

Think beyond the game

Games teach us a little about life.

When we play, we practise taking turns, waiting, losing without being unkind and cheering for someone else. These are useful skills.

It is also okay to say, “I do not want to play this one.” Choosing is part of being a good friend.

Talk / Wonder / Make

What do you think?

  1. Talk. Which game from your childhood would you teach to a new friend?
  2. Wonder. Why do you think similar games appear in so many places?
  3. Make. Write the rules of a game in three sentences. Ask someone to try it.

Nothing you say or draw is collected here.

It’s okay to take a break.

If a game feels too fast or noisy, it is okay to sit out and join again later.

For families and educators

Game names and rules vary by region, family and school. Treat the names here as examples. Children may know others, and that is a good chance to share.

Origins of games are often told as stories without proof. The Roman-origin story for hopscotch, for example, is not supported by evidence, so this issue does not claim it.
