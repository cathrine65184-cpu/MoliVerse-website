Why Are There So Many Languages?

A world question to wonder about together.

English, with a little Spanish · Read together with a parent or educator

Before we begin

This issue is a question without one right answer. Read with someone and wonder out loud.

A letter to our readers

Hi, children.

Hi, children. Our week of Spanish ends with a big question: why do people speak so many different languages?

We will not find one final answer, but we will find some interesting clues.

A word we can explain

A language is a system of words and rules for sharing ideas.

People today speak more than 7,000 languages. Some have hundreds of millions of speakers, and some have only a few. More than 500 million people speak Spanish as a first language, and about 635 million speak it in all.

Languages change slowly over time, a bit like family traditions that shift from one generation to the next.

An idea to wonder about

Languages have families.

Spanish, French, Portuguese, Italian and Romanian all grew from Latin, so many of their words look alike. “Mother” is madre in Spanish and Italian, mère in French and mãe in Portuguese.

Languages also borrow. English uses Spanish words such as patio, tornado, canyon, mosquito and plaza. Spanish uses Arabic words such as azúcar (sugar), aceituna (olive) and ojalá (hopefully).

Think beyond the words

Every language holds a way of seeing.

Each language has its own sayings, jokes and ways of describing the world. Learning even a few words is a way of showing, “I am interested in you.”

About four in ten languages are endangered, often because very few people still use them. When a language fades, stories and knowledge can fade with it. That is one reason people work to keep them alive.

A small word notebook

Open a word when you need it.

language

words and rules that people use to talk and write

native speaker

a person who learned a language from early childhood

borrow

to take a word from another language

translate

to change words from one language into another

accent

the way a person’s speech sounds, often linked to where they come from

family of languages

a group of languages that grew from the same older language

Talk / Wonder / Make

What do you think?

  1. Talk. Which languages are spoken in your home, school or neighbourhood?
  2. Wonder. Why might people borrow words from each other?
  3. Make. Pick one word from your language and make a “word travel card”. Teach it to someone.

Nothing you say or draw is collected here.

It’s okay to take a break.

Making mistakes in a new language is normal. Everyone who learns a language does it.

For families and educators

Language counts and speaker numbers vary by source and year, so treat them as approximate. The Latin-family and borrowed-word examples are standard linguistics facts, but we did not check them against a specific online source.

This is a good moment to invite children to share the languages they speak or hear, and to treat each of them as a strength.
